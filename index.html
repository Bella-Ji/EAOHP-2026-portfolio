How Workplace Mistreatment Undermines Next-Day Work Meaningfulness: The Role of Emotional Exhaustion and Person-Level Job Burnout

Daily work meaningfulness is actively constructed through the investment of psychological resources, and is therefore vulnerable to experiences that deplete them.
01 Background & Hypotheses
  • Work meaningfulness — a central component of eudaimonic well-being — is not given but actively constructed through meaning-making, the ongoing investment of psychological resources.
  • Workplace experiences that deplete these resources therefore reduce the daily capacity to sustain it.
  • Workplace mistreatment is a prevalent interpersonal stressor that depletes such resources, leading to emotional exhaustion.
  • Emotional exhaustion serves as a mediating mechanism linking mistreatment to lower work meaningfulness (JD-R theory).
  • Person-level job burnout moderates this mediation: the negative indirect effect is amplified among chronically depleted individuals (COR theory).

04 Discussion

01. Daily interpersonal stressors as antecedents of work meaningfulness

Work meaningfulness is not a static attribute of job, but an experience actively constructed in daily work life. Our findings identify daily workplace mistreatment as a previously overlooked antecedent, shifting attention toward what employees encounter day-to-day.

02. Resource-based pathway linking mistreatment to work meaningfulness

Empirical evidence for the resource-dependent nature of work meaningfulness has been scarce. When mistreatment depletes employees' psychological resources, their capacity to invest in meaning construction is diminished — extending JD-R's health impairment process to eudaimonic outcomes.

03. Person-level burnout amplifies the second stage, not the first

Heightened vulnerability of depleted individuals does not operate by amplifying acute (first-stage) resource loss itself. Rather, it reflects a diminished capacity to invest remaining resources in constructing eudaimonic well-being such as work meaningfulness.
